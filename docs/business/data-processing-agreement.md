# Data Processing Agreement

> **Draft prepared without legal review. Have a UAE-qualified lawyer review before signing.**

**Schedule 2 to the Master Services Agreement** reference [agreement number] between:

- **Al Durrah International School LLC**, Sharjah, United Arab Emirates (the **Customer** or **Controller**); and
- **Nexus AI Assurance FZCO**, [free zone], United Arab Emirates, +971 52 377 7246 (the **Supplier** or **Processor**).

This Data Processing Agreement (**DPA**) applies to all Personal Data the Supplier Processes for the Customer when providing Horizon under the Master Services Agreement (the **MSA**). It is written to meet UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data (the **PDPL**).

**Note for review:** the lawyer should confirm the status of the PDPL executive regulations at the date of signature and update the references to them; check whether any SPEA or Ministry of Education rule on student data adds requirements; and check whether UAE Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the use of information and communications technology in health fields applies to the school clinic's records (see clause 11.6).

## 1. Definitions

1.1 Terms defined in the MSA have the same meaning here. In addition:

- **Personal Data**, **Sensitive Personal Data**, **Processing**, **Controller**, **Processor**, **Data Subject** and **Personal Data Breach** have the meanings given in the PDPL.
- **Data Office** means the UAE Data Office or any other authority that supervises the PDPL.
- **Children's Data** means Personal Data about students, nearly all of whom are under 18.
- **Sensitive Records** means wellbeing and counselling records, safeguarding (child protection) records, medical and health information, special educational needs information, and identity document numbers.
- **Safeguarding Records** means child protection cases, concerns, notes, chronologies, external referrals and decisions about informing families, held in the Platform's safeguarding area.
- **Sub-processor** means a third party the Supplier engages to Process Customer Personal Data.
- **Customer Personal Data** means Personal Data in the Customer Data.
- **Instructions** has the meaning in clause 3.

## 2. Roles

2.1 For Customer Personal Data, the Customer is the Controller and the Supplier is the Processor.

2.2 The Supplier is a Controller only for the limited business contact details it holds to manage the contract (for example the names, work emails and phone numbers of the Customer's contacts and invoice recipients), and for its own security and operations logs that do not contain Customer Personal Data. That data is outside this DPA.

2.3 The Customer is responsible for having a lawful basis for the Processing it instructs, for giving Data Subjects the privacy information required by the PDPL, and for collecting consent where consent is the basis (including consent from a parent or legal guardian for a child, where needed).

## 3. Instructions

3.1 The Supplier Processes Customer Personal Data only on the Customer's documented Instructions, unless UAE law requires otherwise. In that case the Supplier will tell the Customer before Processing, unless the law prohibits it.

3.2 The Customer's Instructions are:
- (a) the MSA, this DPA and the Order Form;
- (b) the Customer's configuration and use of the Platform by its Authorised Users, including the settings its administrators choose in the Compliance area (processing purposes, consent, retention, approved processors, cross-border transfer decision, AI settings and identity document policy);
- (c) any other written instruction from the Customer's data protection lead or Head of IT that is consistent with the MSA.

3.3 The Supplier will tell the Customer promptly if, in its opinion, an Instruction breaches the PDPL. The Supplier is not required to follow an Instruction that it reasonably believes is unlawful.

## 5. Supplier obligations

The Supplier will:

5.1 **Purpose.** Process Customer Personal Data only to provide the Platform and the services under the MSA, and not for its own purposes. It will not sell Customer Personal Data, use it for advertising, or use it to train artificial intelligence models.

5.2 **Personnel.** Make sure that everyone it authorises to Process Customer Personal Data is bound by a written duty of confidentiality, has received data protection training, and has access only to what they need for their role. The names of the Supplier personnel who hold administrative access to the production systems are listed in Annex 5 and will be kept up to date.

5.3 **Access to school data by the Supplier.** Supplier personnel do not have user access inside the Customer's school in the Platform unless the Customer invites them, for example during setup. Such access is given through a normal user account with a role the Customer chooses, every action is recorded in the Customer's audit log, and the Customer removes it when it is no longer needed. Supplier personnel may access production systems directly (for example the database) only to operate the service, to investigate an incident, or at the Customer's request, and will keep a record of each such access that touches Customer Personal Data and share it with the Customer on request.

5.4 **Security.** Implement and maintain the technical and organisational measures in Annex 2, and not materially reduce them during the term.

5.5 **Records.** Keep a record of the Processing it carries out for the Customer, as the PDPL requires of processors, and make it available to the Customer on request.

5.6 **Assistance.** Help the Customer, taking into account the nature of the Processing, to meet its PDPL obligations on security, Personal Data Breach notification, data protection impact assessments and responses to the Data Office.

5.7 **Data protection contact.** Give the Customer a named data protection contact (Annex 5). [Where the PDPL requires the Supplier to appoint a data protection officer, the Supplier has appointed [name].]

5.8 **Requests from authorities.** If the Supplier receives a request from a court, authority or other third party for Customer Personal Data, it will refer the request to the Customer and not disclose the data, unless the law requires disclosure. If disclosure is required, it will tell the Customer first where the law allows, and disclose only the minimum required.

## 6. Sub-processors

6.1 **General authorisation.** The Customer authorises the Supplier to use the Sub-processors listed in Annex 3, for the purposes and in the locations stated there. In a new school, the Platform also lists the processors in the Compliance area as "not approved" so the Customer can record its own review and approval.

6.2 **Changes.** The Supplier will give the Customer at least [30] days written notice before adding or replacing a Sub-processor, with its name, service, location and the Personal Data involved.

6.3 **Objection.** The Customer may object in writing within that period on reasonable data protection grounds. The parties will then discuss in good faith. If they cannot agree, the Customer may terminate the affected Module without penalty, and the Supplier refunds the Fees paid in advance for the period after termination. For optional Sub-processors (marked "Optional" in Annex 3), the Customer may instead simply keep the related feature switched off.

6.4 **Flow-down.** The Supplier will have a written contract with each Sub-processor that imposes data protection obligations no less protective than this DPA, as far as they apply to the service provided.

6.5 **Liability.** The Supplier remains responsible to the Customer for the performance of its Sub-processors.

6.6 **Customer's own providers.** Services the Customer chooses to connect, such as its Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or ClassLink tenant used for sign-in, its own student information system, or its own WhatsApp Business account, are provided to the Customer under the Customer's own agreements. They are not Sub-processors of the Supplier.

## 7. Cross-border transfers

7.1 **Location.** The primary copy of Customer Personal Data (database and stored files) is held in the location stated in Annex 3. The Supplier will not move it to another country without the Customer's prior written approval.

7.2 **Transfers.** The Supplier will transfer Customer Personal Data outside the UAE only:
- (a) to the Sub-processors and locations in Annex 3 that the Customer has approved; and
- (b) in a way the PDPL allows, which means either to a country that the UAE has recognised as providing an adequate level of protection, or, where that is not the case, under one of the conditions the PDPL allows for transfers without adequate protection, such as a contract binding the recipient to protection standards equivalent to the PDPL.

7.3 **Safeguards.** For each transfer the Supplier will:
- (a) have the contract described in clause 6.4, with terms that require the recipient to protect the data to a standard equivalent to the PDPL;
- (b) send only the Personal Data the feature needs (for example, an email provider receives the recipient's address and the notification text, never case notes);
- (c) protect the data in transit with encryption;
- (d) keep the Annex 3 list current, including each recipient's country and safeguard.

7.4 **The Customer's switches.** The Platform's Compliance area records the Customer's decision on cross-border transfers (off by default) and lets the Customer switch off optional features that involve a transfer: AI drafting entirely, sensitive AI processing (off by default), WhatsApp (off unless the Customer enables it), and phone app alerts (each person opts in). The cross-border setting records the Customer's decision; the providers actually used are set by the Supplier's configuration and these feature switches. The Supplier will not enable an optional Sub-processor for the Customer's school without the Customer's approval.

7.5 **Note for review:** if the hosting region in Annex 3 is outside the UAE, every item of Customer Personal Data is transferred abroad. The lawyer should confirm which PDPL transfer condition the parties rely on, and whether any data (for example health data, see clause 11.6) must stay in the UAE.

## 8. Personal Data Breaches

8.1 **Notice to the Customer.** The Supplier will notify the Customer without undue delay, and in any case within 72 hours, after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Customer Personal Data. Notice goes by phone and email to the Customer's breach contacts in Annex 5. [The Customer may wish to agree a shorter period, for example 24 hours, for breaches involving Safeguarding Records or medical data.]

8.2 **Content.** The notice will include, as far as then known: what happened and when; the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects and records concerned; whether Sensitive Records or Children's Data are involved; the likely consequences; the measures taken or proposed to contain it and reduce harm; and a contact for more information. Information not available at first will be provided in stages, without undue delay.

8.3 **Cooperation.** The Supplier will investigate, contain and remedy the breach; keep the Customer informed; preserve evidence; and help the Customer meet its own duties to notify the Data Office and affected Data Subjects. The Customer decides whether and how to notify them. The Supplier will not notify the Data Office, Data Subjects or the media about a breach of Customer Personal Data on the Customer's behalf without the Customer's approval, unless the law requires it.

8.4 **Record.** The Customer can record the breach in the Platform's breach log (Compliance area) with severity, dates, number affected and reporting status. The Supplier keeps its own incident record and will share the post-incident report with the Customer.

8.5 Notifying or reporting a breach is not an admission of fault.

## 9. Data Subject rights

9.1 **Tools.** The Platform gives the Customer tools to handle Data Subject requests itself, in its Compliance area:
- (a) a request register for access, correction and deletion requests about students, guardians and staff, with a due date (30 days by default, which the Customer can change) and a recorded resolution;
- (b) correction directly in the records, and re-imports that update rather than duplicate;
- (c) a one-person export: a ZIP file with a summary in English and Arabic, the data as JSON and CSV per area, the person's uploaded files, and a list of what was withheld;
- (d) erasure with a preview of exactly what will be deleted, anonymised or kept under the Customer's retention settings, and a typed confirmation.

9.2 **Protective rules in the tools.** Safeguarding content is never released through a personal data export; the Designated Safeguarding Lead decides on any disclosure outside the Platform. Wellbeing and medical content is included only for a logged access request and only when the person exporting is allowed to see it. Withheld sensitive records are counted under one general "Restricted records" line, so an export does not reveal that a safeguarding case exists. Identity numbers appear with the last four digits only. Records the Customer's retention settings require it to keep are anonymised rather than deleted, and Safeguarding Records are never deleted by an erasure.

9.3 **Assistance.** If the Customer needs help beyond these tools, the Supplier will assist within [5] Business Days of a written request.

9.4 **Requests to the Supplier.** If a Data Subject contacts the Supplier directly about Customer Personal Data, the Supplier will pass the request to the Customer within [2] Business Days and will not respond to it except to say it has been passed on, unless the Customer instructs otherwise.

## 10. Retention and deletion

10.1 **During the term.** The Customer sets retention periods per record type in the Compliance area. A nightly background job applies them. Deletion is applied automatically only where the policy says delete (by default, in-app notifications and AI drafts). Review and anonymise policies produce counts for a person at the school to act on; they do not change records by themselves. Safeguarding Records are review-only and are never deleted automatically. The default periods, which the Customer should confirm against SPEA and Ministry of Education requirements, are in Annex 1.

10.2 **At the end.** On termination or expiry, the Supplier returns and deletes Customer Personal Data as set out in clause 19 of the MSA: an export in standard formats within 30 days, deletion from the live Platform, a signed Deletion Certificate, and expiry of backup copies within [backup retention period].

10.3 **Legal retention.** The Supplier keeps no Customer Personal Data after deletion, except copies in backups until they expire, and where UAE law requires the Supplier itself to keep it, in which case the Supplier will tell the Customer what is kept and why, and continue to protect it under this DPA.

## 11. Children's data and safeguarding records

11.1 **Children first.** The Supplier recognises that nearly all students are children and that Customer Personal Data includes Sensitive Records about them. It will apply the extra protections in this clause on top of the rest of this DPA.

11.2 **Safeguarding Records.**
- (a) **One access module.** Safeguarding and wellbeing records are reached only through the Platform's single case access module. They never appear in global search, dashboards, analytics, school group dashboards, or the parent and student portals, unless that module allows it.
- (b) **Who can see them.** Full safeguarding access is limited to the Designated Safeguarding Lead and deputy, the assigned case owner and staff given an explicit access grant. A member of staff who raises a concern sees only its status. Time-limited emergency access requires a written reason and alerts the Designated Safeguarding Lead.
- (c) **Audit.** Every view of a safeguarding, wellbeing or confidential case writes an entry in the audit log.
- (d) **Families are never notified automatically.** Informing a family about a wellbeing or safeguarding matter is a recorded staff decision with a reason. Phone and WhatsApp alerts about such matters never carry the content, only a generic line and a link that needs sign-in.
- (e) **Immutable history.** Case notes can be amended but not deleted, and each amendment keeps the earlier version. Emergency access records and family notification decisions cannot be changed or deleted by the application.
- (f) **Supplier personnel.** Supplier personnel will not open the content of Safeguarding Records, including through direct database access, except where strictly necessary to resolve an incident or a support request that the Customer has made in writing, with the Designated Safeguarding Lead informed in advance (or as soon as possible in an emergency). Each such access will be recorded and reported to the Customer.
- (g) **AI.** Safeguarding, wellbeing and medical information is never sent to an AI provider unless the Customer switches on sensitive AI processing, which needs a recorded reason and is audited. The default is off. Any AI output is a draft that a staff member must review.
- (h) **Export.** Only the Designated Safeguarding Lead can export a safeguarding chronology. On exit, Safeguarding Records are released only as set out in clause 19.3 of the MSA.
- (i) **Disclosure to authorities.** Requests from the Child Protection Unit, police or other authorities for Safeguarding Records are handled by the Customer. If such a request reaches the Supplier, clause 5.8 applies.

11.3 **Minimisation.** The Supplier will not ask the Customer to enter more Personal Data than a feature needs. Identity document numbers are optional per school policy (off, optional or required) and are encrypted.

11.4 **Notifications to children.** Notifications are written so that they do not reveal Sensitive Records, and message notifications never contain the message text.

11.5 **Reporting duties.** The Customer remains responsible for reporting duties under UAE Federal Law No. 3 of 2016 on Child Rights. The Platform's external referral log supports the Customer's record of those reports.

11.6 **Medical data.** The Platform stores students' allergies, conditions and medications encrypted, and the reasons for medical absences are visible only to the family and to staff holding the attendance reasons or medical permissions (by default the school administrator, the registrar and the school nurse). **Note for review:** UAE Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the use of information and communications technology in health fields may restrict storing health data outside the UAE. The lawyer should confirm whether it applies to the school clinic's records. If it does and the hosting region in Annex 3 is outside the UAE, the Customer should not enter clinic records in the Platform until the data is hosted in the UAE.

## 12. Audits and information

12.1 **Information.** On request, and at least once a year, the Supplier will give the Customer the information reasonably needed to show compliance with this DPA: the current Security Overview, answers to a security questionnaire, the Sub-processor list, and the summary of the most recent independent penetration test once one has been done.

12.2 **Audit.** If that information is not enough to show compliance, or after a Personal Data Breach, or if the Data Office requires it, the Customer may audit the Supplier's compliance with this DPA, once a year unless a breach or the Data Office requires otherwise. The Customer gives at least [30] days notice, the audit is carried out by the Customer or an independent auditor bound by confidentiality, during business hours, without access to other schools' data, and at the Customer's cost, unless it reveals a material breach by the Supplier, in which case the Supplier bears its reasonable cost.

12.3 **Sub-processors.** Audits of Sub-processors are done by relying on their published certifications and audit reports, where available.

## 13. Liability

13.1 Each party's liability under this DPA is subject to the limits and exclusions in clause 16 of the MSA [including the data protection cap in clause 16.3, if the parties include it].

## 14. Term

14.1 This DPA lasts as long as the Supplier Processes Customer Personal Data, including during the Exit Period and until deletion under clause 10.2 is complete.

## 15. Order of precedence

15.1 For anything about Personal Data, this DPA prevails over the rest of the MSA.

## Annex 2: Technical and organisational security measures

These measures describe what the Platform does today. The Security Overview gives more detail and lists known gaps.

### Access and isolation

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Tenant isolation | Every school table carries the school's identifier and is protected by PostgreSQL row-level security. Every request runs inside a database transaction that first sets the school's identifier, and the policies allow only that school's rows. The application connects as a separate database role that does not own the tables and cannot bypass these rules. Writes that name a different school are blocked. An automated test suite checks against a real database that one school can never read, insert, change or delete another school's rows. |
| Role-based access | 14 built-in roles (school administrator, principal, registrar, head of department, teacher, counselor, career advisor, wellbeing lead, Designated Safeguarding Lead and deputy, nurse, IT support, student and parent), which the school can copy, rename and extend. Every page and action checks permissions on the server, read from the database on each request, so changes apply on the next click. |
| Sensitive permissions | Granting safeguarding, wellbeing, medical, identity number, audit log or role management permissions requires typing the role name to confirm. An Access check screen shows what any person can see and which role grants it. At least one active administrator must always keep role management. |
| Family scoping | Parents see only their linked children; students see only themselves. Joining with wrong details reveals nothing, and repeated wrong attempts are locked out. |
| Case access module | Wellbeing and safeguarding records are reached through one access module, as described in clause 11.2. |
| Private absence reasons | Reasons and doctor's notes for absences are visible only to the family, holders of the attendance reasons permission (school administrator, registrar, nurse) and medical staff. Teachers see "Excused absence, reported by parent". |
| Suspension | Suspending a person ends their open session at their next page load. |

### Authentication

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Sign-in methods | Email and password, Google, and Microsoft Entra ID. Google and Microsoft sign-in only work for people the school has invited or imported. |
| Passwords | Stored only as bcrypt hashes. |
| Sessions | Signed session tokens that expire after 12 hours. |
| Rate limits | Password attempts are limited per email address and per network address; join codes, invitations and sign-up are also rate limited. Error messages do not reveal whether an account exists. |

### Encryption

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| In transit | The Platform is served over HTTPS. Stored files are downloaded through signed HTTPS links that expire after five minutes. |
| Field-level | Emirates ID and passport numbers, medical fields (allergies, conditions, medications) and the credentials the school stores for scheduled data sync are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key held outside the database. |
| At rest | Provided by the database host and the file storage provider: [confirm at-rest encryption for each provider]. |
| Secrets | School API keys are shown once and stored only as a hash. Invitation and verification tokens are stored as hashes. |

### Files

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Private storage | Files are stored privately in a folder per school and are never public. Every download passes a permission check, and opening a sensitive document or a request attachment is recorded in the audit log. |
| Upload limits | Uploads are limited to 10 MB and to common document and image types. |

### Logging and monitoring

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Audit log | Append-only: the application's database role cannot change or delete audit events. It records invitations and joining, role and permission changes, every view of a sensitive record, every identity number reveal, exports and downloads, AI setting changes and data subject request actions. Authorised staff can filter and export it; the export is itself logged. |
| Application logs | Contain identifiers and counts only, never student personal data. |
| Availability | A status page checks the web application, database, background queue and worker every five minutes and shows 90 days of history. An automated check signs in to the demonstration school every morning and opens every page for every role in English and Arabic. |

### AI

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| One provider module | All AI calls go through one module, and every call is recorded, including calls that were blocked. |
| Sensitive data | Not sent unless the school switches on sensitive AI processing with a recorded reason (default off). |
| Drafts only | AI output is always a draft a person reviews. Eligibility for university programmes is decided by rules, never by AI. |
| Switch off | AI can be switched off for the whole school. Without an external AI provider configured, a built-in drafter works from the same facts with no network call. |

### Messaging

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Minimal content | Notifications about requests never contain message text. Phone alerts and WhatsApp messages about confidential, medical, wellbeing or safeguarding matters carry only a generic line and a link that needs sign-in. |
| Opt-in | Phone app alerts are opt-in per device. WhatsApp is off unless the school enables it per message type, and each parent opts in with their own number and an explicit consent, which they can withdraw. |
| Idempotent delivery | Each message has an idempotency key so it is sent at most once. |

### Integrations

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| School API keys | Created by a school administrator with per-area scopes, limited to 120 requests a minute, revocable at any time, acting with the creator's current permissions. Use is recorded in the audit log. |
| Scheduled sync | Reads the school's export over HTTPS only, refuses private and internal network addresses, follows no redirects and limits file size. Errors never repeat the data sent. |

### Resilience

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Backups | [Backup frequency], kept for [backup retention period], stored in [backup location]. Restore tested [restore test frequency]. |
| Recovery targets | Recovery point objective [RPO]; recovery time objective [RTO]. |
| Background jobs | Every background job is idempotent, so retries never duplicate a side effect. |

### Organisational measures

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Personnel | Confidentiality undertakings, data protection training, least-privilege access to production systems (Annex 5). |
| Secure development | Changes are type-checked and covered by automated unit, integration and end-to-end tests, including the tenant isolation suite and safeguarding access tests. Secrets are kept in the hosting provider's environment settings and never committed to source code. |
| Incident response | Incidents are triaged as Priority 1 under the Service Level Agreement and notified under clause 8. |
